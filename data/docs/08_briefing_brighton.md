### Pre-Match Briefing: Brighton & Hove Albion (Matchday 29)
**Date:** 12 March 2025  
**Author:** Marcus Quell (Lead Opposition Analyst)  
**Audience:** First-Team Coaching Staff  

Brighton & Hove Albion present a unique tactical challenge, building with a back four and a double pivot while pushing both wide players high, a structure that invites aggressive opposition pressing. They deliberately hold the ball deep within their own penalty area, using their goalkeeper as an active participant in the build-up.

This extreme patience is reflected in their build-up statistics; they average a remarkably low 1.4 metres per second in vertical progression speed during their initial build-up phase. On defensive set-pieces, they employ a highly coordinated offside trap, with their entire defensive line stepping up simultaneously precisely as the ball is struck.

To disrupt their rhythm, I advise against deploying a high press. Instead, we should sit in a compact 4-4-2 mid-block, denying access to their central pivots and forcing them to play aimless long balls. When attacking their set-piece trap, we must position our deepest runner on the blindside of their full-back, timing the run from deep to exploit the space behind their advancing line.
